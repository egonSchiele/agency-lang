Checked on 2026-10-23. Every page below loaded without a login, so the

pages as they stood that day. The judge uses this file to check facts and

## Long-form sources

These are the interviews a well-sourced answer should quote. The
sourcing checklist asks for at least two of them.

1. Rolling Stone, "Radiohead: Making Music That Matters", David Fricke,
   2 August 2001.
   https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/radiohead-making-music-that-matters-84574/
   - Colin Greenwood "has known Yorke since they were both twelve, taking
     classical-guitar lessons at Abingdon School near Oxford."
   - "Yorke declares that he knew, at the age of eight, that he would be a
     rock star. ... 'I decided that,' he says, 'when I saw [Queen
     guitarist] Brian May for the first time on TV.'"
   - "Yorke took the time to complete his studies in art and literature at
     Exeter University, leaving with a bachelor of arts degree."
   - "'My principal thing was still music,' Yorke says, dismissing his
     degree as 'bloody useless for anything. But if all I learned the whole
     time I was there was to keep working when you're stuck, well, that's
     all I need to know.'"
   - "'The National Anthem,' on Kid A, is based on a riff that Yorke wrote
     when he was sixteen."
   - On making Kid A: "'I thought chords were boring,' he snaps. 'If anyone
     was playing a straight beat on a snare drum, I was like, "Fuck
     this."'"
   - Colin Greenwood on "Like Spinning Plates": "Thom learned to sing the
     backward melody forward."
   - "Jonny left Oxford Polytechnic in his first year when the band signed
     to EMI in late '91."

2. The Observer (on theguardian.com), "All messed up", Craig McLean,
   18 June 2006.
   https://www.theguardian.com/music/2006/jun/18/9
   - "At the age of seven, inspired by Queen's Brian May, he got his mitts
     on his first guitar."
   - "he finally settled at Abingdon School in Oxfordshire, where along
     with friends, he formed On A Friday, a band named after that sole day
     when they were allowed to practise."
   - "during his art and literature degree at Exeter"; "Yorke and the
     artist [Stanley Donwood] first met at Exeter University, where Yorke
     studied English and Art."
   - On The Eraser: "'I was excited about the idea of just using beats and
     stuff and some of these sounds that I had. And writing to that rather
     than writing to good old-fashioned acoustic instruments [and the idea
     that] it's not a song unless it's got a fucking guitar in it or
     whatever nonsense.'"
   - "five operations in the first five years of his life to repair a
     paralysed left eye"

3. The Guardian, "I think I'm meant to be dead ...", Lauren Zoric,
   22 September 2000 (Kid A interview).
   https://www.theguardian.com/friday_review/story/0,,371289,00.html
   - "much has been made of Kid A's electronic sound, and Yorke's
     embracing of the Warp back catalogue - Autechre, Aphex Twin."
   - Describes the band's aim "to work more like programmers and end up
     with a set of sounds and textures."

## Shorter sources

4. The Guardian, "What were today's celebrities like as children?",
   13 September 2008. Terence Gilmore-James, director of music at Abingdon
   School 1980-1987, on Yorke.
   https://www.theguardian.com/lifeandstyle/2008/sep/13/celebrity.television
   - "I think he found a haven in the music room."
   - "He was a thinker and an experimenter. He wasn't a great musician -
     unlike Jonny [Greenwood, Radiohead's guitarist], for instance -
     although I think it's a slight exaggeration when he says he can't read
     music. He said he couldn't sing, but he has a great way of using his
     voice."

5. BBC, "Ten things we learned from Thom Yorke's Desert Island Discs",
   2019.
   https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/articles/b7zfNZjlKBK3r8s9t9ldhx/ten-things-we-learned-from-thom-yorkes-desert-island-discs
   - "The music school and the art school was my sanctuary."
   - He credits two teachers at his school with setting him on his path:
     "Two of these teachers - the head of the art department and the head
     of the music department - saw something in me and were incredibly
     supportive."
   - On his art degree: "the art college thing just blew my mind and
     without those three years I wouldn't have been creatively prepared
     for what happened after".
   - On his eye: "When I was born, the left eye was shut: there was no
     muscle that would open it."

6. Wikipedia, "Thom Yorke". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thom_Yorke
   - Repeats the facts above with citations: Exeter, "a 2:1 in English and
     art"; "considered studying music but could not read sheet music";
     Gilmore-James's "not a great musician" line; the Brian May story; met
     Stanley Donwood at Exeter. A good answer may use it, but the sourcing
     checklist requires at least one source beyond it.

7. Wikipedia, "Jonny Greenwood".
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonny_Greenwood
   - "Greenwood's first instrument was a recorder given to him at age four
     or five. He played baroque music in recorder groups as a teenager".
   - "He played the viola in the Thames Vale youth orchestra, which he
     described as a formative experience".
   - "He enrolled at Oxford Brookes University to study psychology and
     music, but left after his first term after On a Friday signed a record
     contract deal with EMI."

## Facts the checklist expects

- Degree: English (literature) and art at the University of Exeter. Not
  music. (Sources 1, 2, 6.)
- Got a guitar as a child, inspired by Brian May. The Observer says he was
  seven when he got the guitar; Rolling Stone says he was eight when he saw
  May on TV and decided to be a rock star. Either age is fine. (Sources 1,
  2.)
- Classical guitar lessons at Abingdon School, where he met Colin
  Greenwood. (Source 1.)
- Says he cannot read music; his school music director called that a
  slight exaggeration. (Sources 4, 6.)
- The music and art departments at Abingdon as his refuge. (Sources 4, 5.)
- Turn to electronic music around Kid A under Warp Records influence
  (Autechre, Aphex Twin); later solo work written to beats on a laptop.
  (Sources 2, 3.)
- Jonny Greenwood is the bandmate with the formal musical knowledge: a
  recorder player and youth-orchestra violist who enrolled to study music
  at Oxford Brookes, and the one his school's music director held up
  against Yorke. (Sources 1, 4, 7.)
- Where his strengths lie. No source ranks them, so the point passes when
  the answer takes a position and grounds it, whatever the position. What
  the sources offer: his school music director on his voice, "He said he
  couldn't sing, but he has a great way of using his voice" (source 4);
  his own turn from chords and straight beats on Kid A (source 1) and to
  "writing to" beats on a laptop for The Eraser (source 2); the band
  working "like programmers" on Kid A (source 3); Colin Greenwood on his
  ear, "Thom learned to sing the backward melody forward" (source 1); and
  the arrangement and theory side resting with Jonny Greenwood (sources 4,
  7). An answer that skips the question fails the point.
