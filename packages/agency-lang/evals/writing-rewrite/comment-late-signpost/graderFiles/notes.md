Context: a doc comment on an eval grader. The grader checks that a research agent answered a question in the light of the conversation before it. A contributor reads it to learn what the grader measures.

- "A question whose literal reading and its reading in the conversation differ" holds its point until the last word. "differ" is the one word that says what the sentence is about, and it comes at the very end, so the reader carries the whole sentence without knowing where it is going. Move the signpost forward: "A question whose meaning changes when it is read by itself versus in the context of the full conversation."
- "The answer must use the conversation" treats the answer as a person taking an action, and "use the conversation" confuses more than it clarifies. Say what should be visible in the response: it should show that the question was considered with the rest of the conversation in mind.
- "The answer must use the conversation: a constraint fixed in an earlier turn still holds in the last one." makes the reader read past the colon to learn what the first half meant. The first half should say its point on its own, so nothing after a colon is needed to explain it.
- "a constraint fixed in an earlier turn still holds in the last one" is awkwardly phrased. "fixed" reads as "repaired", and "the last one" needs the reader to recover "turn". Drop it once the first half says the point.
