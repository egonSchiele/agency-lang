Context: a doc comment on an eval grader. The grader checks the citations in a research agent's answer. A contributor reads it to learn what the grader fails.

- "Every URL the answer cites" drops the "that": write "Every URL that the answer cites". Without it the reader briefly parses "the answer" as the object of "URL".
- "must resolve" stops the sentence short. The reader is left asking "resolve to what?" Finish it: "must resolve to a real URL".
- "An answer with no URL fails" delivers its verb abruptly; "fails" lands with no run-up. "is a failure" is a gentler ending.
- "fails: the researcher's contract is a cited answer" has its logic backwards. The reason comes after the colon as a reveal, and the reader has to work out that the rule causes the failure. Put the rule first and the consequence second: "The researcher MUST cite sources, so an answer with no sources is a failure."
- "the researcher's contract is a cited answer" is a metaphor where a plain rule is needed. Say "The researcher MUST cite sources."
